When Attributes Disagree:
Gradient Conflict in Image Aesthetic Assessment

AGREE: Attribute-guided Gradient Routing for Establishing Agreement

Ye Wang1   Maocai Dai1   Jiang Xie1   Xiuli Bi1   Fei Tao2   Xiao Li3   Hong Yu1
1 Chongqing University of Posts and Telecommunications    2 NewsBreak    3 Hong Kong Polytechnic University / University of Oxford
Paper Code BibTeX

Abstract

Image Aesthetic Assessment (IAA) predicts an image's overall aesthetic score, yet aesthetics is influenced by multiple attributes whose relative importance varies with image content and usage scenarios. Under end-to-end training with only overall-score supervision, attribute signals are blended, which can cause gradient conflict across samples dominated by different attributes, resulting in gradient cancellation and persistent systematic bias. To address these issues, we propose AGREE (Attribute-guided Gradient Routing for Establishing Agreement), which learns attribute-specific subspaces and performs gradient routing based on sample-wise attribute sensitivity estimated via perturbation analysis. AGREE further reduces feature coupling across attributes with semantic anchors and improves robustness via error-aware reweighting. Experiments on AVA, LAPIS, AADB, TAD66K, and PARA show consistent improvements over diverse IAA baseline models, and AGREE is plug-and-play for existing end-to-end IAA methods without modifying their original architectures.

Key Contributions

  • An optimization-oriented analysis revealing that shared-parameter training leads to persistent, attribute-related biases across IAA models.
  • AGREE, a plug-and-play framework that adapts update allocation to sample-specific attribute relevance without additional supervision.
  • Comprehensive experiments on 5 datasets × 6 baselines demonstrating consistent improvements, especially on hard-to-optimize samples.

Method Overview

AGREE mitigates gradient conflict via four complementary mechanisms operating at different levels.

Figure: Overview of AGREE — a plug-and-play framework with four mechanisms: Attribute-aware Decoupling, Multimodal Fusion, Sensitivity-guided Routing, and Error-aware Reweighting.

1

Attribute Decoupling

Decomposes images into attribute dimensions with separate parameter blocks to reduce coupling.

2

Multimodal Fusion

Injects frozen LLaVA text anchors for semantic separation across attribute branches.

3

Sensitivity Routing

Computes per-sample attribute sensitivity via perturbation to guide feature fusion and update allocation.

4

Error-aware Reweighting

Dynamically upweights hard samples using EMA-tracked prediction errors during training.

Motivation: Gradient Conflict in IAA

Under standard end-to-end training, IAA models exhibit consistent cross-model systematic errors on hard samples. AGREE reveals this is caused by gradient conflict across attribute-dominant subsets and resolves it via attribute-aware optimization.

Left: Baseline models achieve competitive performance on normal samples. Right: All models fail collectively on hard samples — AGREE substantially reduces these shared errors.

Illustration of gradient conflict: when subset gradients point in opposing directions on coupled parameters, they partially cancel, leading to optimization stagnation on hard samples.

Gradient Conflict Analysis

AGREE eliminates inter-attribute gradient conflicts and reduces the conflict ratio by 24%, leading to smoother optimization and faster convergence.

(a) Baseline EAT shows widespread negative cosine similarities (gradient conflict) across attribute pairs. (b) AGREE keeps all off-diagonal entries near zero. (c) AGREE reduces conflict ratio and stabilizes training dynamics.

Plug-and-Play Improvements

AGREE is compatible with diverse IAA architectures. Average SRCC gains of 4.2%–12.6% across datasets, with hard samples improving by up to 38.5%.

Dumbbell plots: each line connects baseline (hollow) to +AGREE (filled). Green = overall results; Red = hard samples. Consistent gains across all six baselines and five datasets.
